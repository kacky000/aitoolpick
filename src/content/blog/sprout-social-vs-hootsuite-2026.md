Sprout Social and Hootsuite are the two tools that come up whenever social media teams at mid-sized or larger companies evaluate platforms. Both are expensive. Both have serious feature sets. And they have meaningfully different strengths that make one or the other the better fit depending on how your team actually works.

This comparison focuses on the decision-relevant differences — not a feature checklist, but where each tool genuinely pulls ahead.

## Pricing: Hootsuite Has the Lower Entry Point

Both tools are per-seat, and both require annual commitment. But Hootsuite starts cheaper.

| Plan | Sprout Social | Hootsuite |
|------|--------------|-----------|
| Entry tier | $199/seat/month | $99/seat/month |
| Mid tier | $299/seat/month | $199/seat/month |
| Advanced | $399/seat/month | $399/seat/month |
| Free trial | 30 days, no credit card | 30 days, credit card required |

For a team of 3:

| Tool | Plan | Monthly Cost |
|------|------|-------------|
| Hootsuite Standard | $99 × 3 | $297/month |
| Hootsuite Professional | $199 × 3 | $597/month |
| Sprout Social Standard | $199 × 3 | $597/month |
| Sprout Social Professional | $299 × 3 | $897/month |

Hootsuite is consistently cheaper at equivalent feature tiers. But whether cheaper is better depends entirely on what you're buying those features for.

## Analytics: Sprout Wins, and It's Not Close

If there's one area where Sprout Social has a clear and consistent edge, it's analytics.

**Sprout Social's reporting** is the best-looking, most detailed, and most presentation-ready output in the social media management space. Reports come with clean visualizations, competitor benchmarking, cross-channel roll-ups, and enough data depth to satisfy most analytics-focused stakeholders. Exporting reports for clients or executives is a routine part of using Sprout — it's built for it.

**Hootsuite's analytics** are functional and have improved significantly, but they still feel like an add-on rather than a core product strength. At the Standard tier, they're basic. At Professional and Advanced, they're useful but not as polished as Sprout's equivalent output.

**Who this matters for:** If your social media manager regularly presents performance data to leadership, clients, or marketing leadership, Sprout saves significant time compared to Hootsuite. If your team primarily uses analytics for internal monitoring without formal reporting, the gap is less meaningful.

**Edge: Sprout Social**, clearly and consistently.

## Social Listening: Hootsuite Includes It Earlier

Both tools have social listening, but they structure access differently.

**Hootsuite** includes social listening starting at its Standard plan (the $99 entry tier), though the feature set expands significantly at Professional and above.

**Sprout Social** locks its listening features to the Advanced plan ($399/seat) or as a paid add-on. At Standard and Professional, listening isn't included.

This means at the $99-$199/seat range, Hootsuite has social listening and Sprout doesn't. If monitoring brand conversations and keywords is a baseline requirement, Hootsuite wins at lower price points.

At the $399/seat tier where both tools offer listening, Sprout's listening tools are considered more sophisticated and better integrated into the broader platform.

**Edge: Hootsuite** for cost-efficient listening access. Sprout at high-end tiers for depth.

## Publishing and Scheduling: Hootsuite Is More Polished Here

This surprises some people, but Hootsuite's publishing tools are more mature than Sprout's.

Hootsuite has better bulk scheduling, a more refined visual content calendar, and a longer history of reliable scheduling across platforms. The CSV import for bulk post creation is straightforward and widely used in agencies managing high content volumes.

Sprout Social's publishing is fine — it covers the basics competently — but it's not where Sprout has invested its development energy. Teams that live in the content creation and scheduling workflow tend to find Hootsuite's tools more natural.

**Edge: Hootsuite** for scheduling-heavy workflows.

## Customer Care and Inbox Management: Sprout Wins

Sprout Social's smart inbox is one of its signature features, and it's genuinely better than Hootsuite's equivalent.

Sprout routes all incoming messages, comments, mentions, and DMs into a unified queue. You can tag messages by type, assign them to team members, set status (open, in progress, closed), and filter by channel or content type. For teams handling customer service through social media, this workflow is well-designed.

The message spike alerts at Advanced tier are particularly useful for customer-facing teams — when your inbox volume suddenly surges (often a sign of a PR issue or viral content), Sprout notifies you in real time.

Hootsuite has an inbox too, and it works, but it's not as refined for customer care workflows. Teams doing high-volume customer engagement on social tend to prefer Sprout.

**Edge: Sprout Social**, especially for customer care-focused teams.

## Platform Support

Both tools support the major platforms: Instagram, Facebook, X (Twitter), LinkedIn, TikTok, Pinterest, YouTube.

Hootsuite has broader platform coverage overall, including some regional networks and additional integrations like Google My Business. Sprout is more focused on the core platforms and tends to have deeper integration with them.

For most teams, this difference doesn't matter — you're using the same 4-5 platforms either way.

**Edge: Hootsuite** for breadth. Negligible for most use cases.

## Ease of Use

Sprout Social has the better interface. The onboarding is thoughtful, the navigation is logical, and new users typically find their footing faster.

Hootsuite works, but it shows its age. The interface is more complex, the settings structure is harder to navigate, and the learning curve is steeper. Teams that live in the tool daily adapt, but the initial experience isn't as smooth.

**Edge: Sprout Social.**

## What Each Tool Is Best For

**Hootsuite fits better when:**
- Budget is a priority and you need a capable tool at a lower entry price
- Your team is publishing-first — high content volume, scheduling is the core workflow
- You need social listening starting at lower price tiers
- You're managing many accounts (agency context) with a small team
- You need platform breadth beyond the core 5-6 channels

**Sprout Social fits better when:**
- Analytics and reporting are central to your team's work
- You present performance data to clients or leadership regularly
- Customer service through social media is a significant part of the workflow
- You need a polished inbox management system for handling high message volumes
- Budget is secondary to quality — you're buying the best tool, not the cheapest

## The Honest Bottom Line

Hootsuite is better value for money at lower price tiers. Sprout Social delivers a premium experience in analytics and inbox management that justifies its higher price for teams that use those features.

The choice usually comes down to one question: is your team's primary social media job **publishing content** or **analyzing performance and managing conversations**?

Publishing-first teams: Hootsuite.

Analytics-first and customer care teams: Sprout Social.

Both tools offer free trials. Hootsuite's trial requires a credit card; Sprout's doesn't. If you're genuinely deciding between the two, start with Sprout's trial (no credit card risk) and build out a real workflow for two weeks. Then trial Hootsuite. The hands-on difference will be clearer than any comparison article can make it.
