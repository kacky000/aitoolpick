# Sourcegraph Cody Pricing 2026: Complete Breakdown

If you are evaluating AI coding assistants for your team, understanding **Cody pricing** is essential before you commit. Sourcegraph Cody stands apart from most competitors because it was built on top of Sourcegraph's code search engine, which means it understands your entire codebase, not just the file open in your editor. In 2026, Sourcegraph has increasingly steered Cody toward enterprise teams that need deep, secure codebase context, and that shift is reflected in how the tiers are structured today.

This guide breaks down every Cody plan, who each one suits, and how Cody stacks up against GitHub Copilot and Cursor.

## What Is Sourcegraph Cody?

Cody is Sourcegraph's AI coding assistant. It provides autocomplete, inline chat, refactoring, unit-test generation, and codebase-wide question answering directly inside VS Code, JetBrains IDEs, and the command line.

The defining feature is **codebase-aware context**. Instead of guessing based on a single file, Cody retrieves relevant code from across your repositories using Sourcegraph's search and embeddings. Ask "where is authentication handled?" and Cody pulls the actual functions from your project rather than inventing a generic answer. For large, unfamiliar, or legacy codebases, this context advantage is Cody's biggest selling point and the reason many platform and enterprise teams pick it.

Cody also lets you choose between multiple underlying LLMs (including Claude and GPT-class models), so you are not locked into a single provider.

## Free vs Pro vs Enterprise

Sourcegraph keeps the lineup simple with three tiers.

**Free** is aimed at individual developers who want to try Cody or use it on personal projects. It includes autocomplete and chat with monthly usage limits, and it works across supported IDEs. It is genuinely usable for light work but the caps become noticeable if you code full time.

**Pro** removes most of the individual limits. You get higher (effectively unlimited for typical use) autocomplete and chat, priority access to premium models, and larger context windows. Pro is the sweet spot for professional solo developers and freelancers.

**Enterprise** is where Sourcegraph now concentrates its attention. This tier unlocks the full codebase-context engine across all your repos, self-hosted or managed deployment options, SSO/SAML, admin controls, audit logging, and guaranteed data privacy (your code is not used for training). The emphasis on enterprise codebase context is a deliberate strategic move: Sourcegraph's core strength is understanding massive multi-repo codebases, and that value is hard for point tools to replicate.

## Who Each Tier Suits

**Choose Free if** you want to test Cody's autocomplete and chat, work on small personal repos, or only need occasional AI help. It is a low-risk way to see whether the codebase-context approach fits your workflow.

**Choose Pro (~$9/user/month) if** you code professionally as an individual and hit the Free limits. At roughly the same price as Copilot's individual plan, Pro gives you unlimited daily use and access to premium models, which pays for itself quickly if AI assistance is part of your routine.

**Choose Enterprise if** you work on a team with multiple large repositories, need security and compliance guarantees, or want AI answers grounded in your actual company codebase. This is where Cody is strongest and where Sourcegraph clearly wants larger customers to land. If codebase context across many repos is your core problem, Enterprise is the reason to pick Cody over anything else.

## Cody vs Copilot vs Cursor

All three tools do autocomplete and chat, but they optimize for different things.

- **GitHub Copilot** is the safe, ubiquitous default. It is deeply integrated with GitHub and priced similarly to Cody Pro, but its context is more focused on the current file and open tabs than on searching an entire multi-repo codebase. For a detailed Copilot cost breakdown, see the [GitHub Copilot Pricing](/blog/github-copilot-pricing-2026) guide.
- **Cursor** is an AI-first editor (a VS Code fork) that many developers love for its fluid multi-file editing and agent features. It is a full IDE rather than a plugin, so adopting it means changing your editor, not just adding an extension.
- **Cody** wins on true codebase-wide context and on enterprise deployment flexibility (including self-hosting). If your pain point is "the AI does not understand our huge codebase," Cody's search-backed retrieval is the differentiator.

## Verdict

Sourcegraph Cody is priced competitively for individuals and increasingly compelling for teams. The **Free** tier is a legitimate way to try before you buy, **Pro at roughly $9/user/month** matches Copilot on cost while offering model choice and strong context, and **Enterprise** is where Cody truly shines thanks to Sourcegraph's deep multi-repo codebase understanding, self-hosting, and security controls.

If you are a solo developer, Pro is easy to justify. If you are an engineering leader wrestling with a sprawling codebase and compliance requirements, Enterprise is arguably the best AI assistant on the market for that specific problem.
