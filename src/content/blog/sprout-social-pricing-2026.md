Sprout Social is expensive. There's no softening that — $199 per seat per month is a serious commitment, and the pricing only goes up from there. The question is whether the feature set justifies the cost for your specific situation.

This is a straightforward breakdown of what each plan actually includes, what's worth paying for, and who should probably look elsewhere.

## Sprout Social Pricing Plans in 2026

All prices are per seat, billed annually. Monthly billing is available at higher rates.

| Plan | Price (Annual) | Social Profiles | Key Features |
|------|---------------|-----------------|-------------|
| Standard | $199/seat/month | 5 included | Publishing, basic analytics, task management |
| Professional | $299/seat/month | 10 included | Competitive reports, scheduling enhancements, more profile slots |
| Advanced | $399/seat/month | 10 included | Social listening, message spike alerts, saved/suggested replies |
| Enterprise | Custom | Custom | Advanced listening, premium analytics, API access, dedicated support |

*Additional social profiles can be added to any plan at extra cost.*

### Standard Plan ($199/seat/month)

The entry point for Sprout gets you 5 social profiles, unlimited publishing, a shared content calendar, a unified smart inbox for managing all your incoming messages, and standard analytics with 180 days of historical data.

The smart inbox is one of Sprout's strongest features — it pulls all your social messages, comments, and mentions into a single queue so your team isn't jumping between platforms. At this tier, it's already functional.

What's missing: competitive benchmarking reports, more advanced scheduling tools, and the social listening features that Sprout is known for. Those are locked to Professional and Advanced respectively.

For context on how this compares to alternatives, see our breakdown of [Sprout Social vs Hootsuite 2026](/blog/sprout-social-vs-hootsuite-2026/).

### Professional Plan ($299/seat/month)

Professional adds competitive comparison reports, trending hashtag data, and the ability to tag incoming messages with custom labels for routing and reporting.

The competitive reports are genuinely valuable if you're doing serious social media strategy work — you can benchmark your performance against named competitors directly within the platform. At Standard, you're flying blind on how you compare.

Scheduling improvements at this tier include content suggestions and optimal timing recommendations based on your audience's activity patterns.

**Who this is for:** Teams where reporting quality matters and who have named competitors they're actively tracking.

### Advanced Plan ($399/seat/month)

The Advanced tier adds Sprout's core social listening feature, which is a meaningful upgrade. Social listening lets you monitor keywords, hashtags, and topics across social platforms — not just your own accounts but across the broader conversation.

Other notable additions at Advanced:
- Message spike alerts (notifies you when incoming message volume surges, useful for catching PR issues early)
- Saved replies for common responses
- Suggested replies using AI
- Enhanced inbox filtering

For teams doing customer care or crisis monitoring, the message spike alerts alone can be worth the upgrade.

**Who this is for:** Teams doing brand monitoring, crisis management, or customer support at scale.

### Enterprise (Custom Pricing)

Enterprise pricing isn't published. Expect to negotiate based on team size, required features, and contract length. Typically this involves premium social listening with larger data volumes, API access, custom onboarding, a dedicated account manager, and SLA guarantees.

For most businesses reading this comparison, Enterprise isn't the relevant tier.

## Add-Ons and Extra Costs

Sprout charges for additional social profiles beyond what's included in your plan. This adds up if you're managing many accounts.

Other notable add-ons (pricing varies):
- **Premium analytics modules**: Deeper reporting on specific platforms (Instagram, LinkedIn, etc.)
- **Sprout Social Influencer** (formerly Tagger): Influencer marketing platform, priced separately
- **Sprout Social Listening**: Listening can be added as a module even below Advanced tier at additional cost

The add-on model means the listed plan price isn't always the total price you'll pay. Factor this in when comparing.

## The 30-Day Free Trial

Sprout offers a 30-day free trial on Standard and Professional with no credit card required. This is one of the better trials in the social media management space — a full month gives you enough time to actually build a workflow and evaluate whether the tool fits.

This is meaningfully different from Hootsuite's trial, which requires a credit card.

## What Sprout Social Does Exceptionally Well

**Analytics and reporting**: Sprout's reports are genuinely impressive. They're detailed, well-designed, and easy to share with clients or executives. This is the area where Sprout most clearly beats competitors. At Professional and above, you're getting reports that look like they were built for boardrooms.

**Unified smart inbox**: Managing social conversations across platforms in one inbox is well-implemented. The tagging, assignment, and filtering tools make it practical for a team to manage high message volumes.

**Customer care workflows**: If part of your social media job is handling customer complaints, questions, and support requests, Sprout is built for this in a way that Buffer and Hootsuite Standard aren't.

**Social listening (Advanced)**: The listening tools let you monitor industry conversations, not just your own brand. For B2B companies and agencies doing strategic research, this has real value.

## Where Sprout Falls Short

**Price**: This is the obvious one. $199/seat/month minimum means a 3-person team is paying $597/month or $7,164/year. For small businesses, this is hard to justify when Buffer covers core scheduling needs at a fraction of the cost.

**Per-seat scaling**: Like Hootsuite, costs scale with team size. A 10-person team on Standard is spending $1,990/month. At that scale, you need to be confident the tool is generating real business value.

**No free plan**: Similar to Hootsuite. The 30-day trial is good, but if you can't pay for it, you can't use it.

**Publishing isn't Sprout's strongest suit**: Paradoxically, for a social media tool, the publishing and scheduling interface isn't as smooth as Buffer's. Sprout's strength is in the analytics and engagement side, not the content creation and scheduling side.

## Who Should Pay for Sprout Social?

**Yes, Sprout makes sense if:**
- You're a marketing team at a mid-market or larger company where analytics depth matters
- Your social media manager's job includes detailed reporting to stakeholders or clients
- You're handling significant customer service volume through social channels
- You need competitive benchmarking as part of your regular workflow
- Social listening is a real business need, not a nice-to-have

**No, Sprout probably isn't worth it if:**
- You're a small business or independent creator
- Your primary need is scheduling content, not analytics
- Budget is constrained — Buffer or a cheaper alternative will cover 80% of needs at 20% of the cost
- You're just getting started and haven't validated that social media drives real business results

## The Bottom Line

Sprout Social at $199/seat/month is genuinely good software — the analytics, reporting, and inbox management are best-in-class. But "genuinely good" only justifies the price if you're using those features consistently.

For teams where the primary need is scheduling content, the price is hard to justify. For teams where reporting, analytics, and customer engagement management are central to the job, Sprout regularly earns back its cost.

The 30-day free trial requires no credit card, so there's no reason not to test it before deciding. Build out two weeks of real workflow in Sprout, then compare what you're actually using against what it costs. That'll tell you more than any comparison article.
