# Kiro Pricing 2026: Complete Breakdown

If you are weighing whether AWS's agentic IDE fits your budget, this guide covers Kiro pricing in full for 2026. Kiro pricing is built around tiered plans measured in agentic interactions (credits), with a genuinely useful free tier and two paid steps for heavier users. Below you'll find a clear pricing table, a free-vs-paid comparison, guidance on who each tier suits, and how Kiro stacks up against other AI coding IDEs.

## What Is Kiro?

Kiro is AWS's agentic AI IDE, launched in preview in 2025. Unlike autocomplete-first tools, Kiro is designed around **spec-driven development**: instead of prompting a model to spit out code, you start with a spec that captures requirements, a design, and a task list. Kiro then turns that spec into working code, keeping the intent and the implementation in sync as your project grows.

Two features define the Kiro experience:

- **Specs** — Structured requirements documents (often written in EARS-style acceptance criteria) that Kiro uses as the source of truth. This makes it far better suited to real features than one-shot code generation, because the plan is reviewable before any code is written.
- **Agent hooks** — Event-driven automations that fire on actions like saving a file, creating a file, or committing. A hook can automatically update tests, refresh documentation, or run a security scan, so quality tasks happen without you remembering to trigger them.

Together, specs and hooks push Kiro toward "software you can reason about" rather than "code that appeared." That positioning matters when you look at pricing, because Kiro meters usage by **agentic interactions** — each substantial agent task consumes credits.

> **Note:** Kiro is evolving quickly out of preview, and interaction limits and prices change. Always confirm the current figures and overage rates on Kiro's official website before committing. The numbers above are realistic 2026 estimates for planning purposes, not a quote.

Kiro's paid plans typically allow **overages** — if you exhaust your monthly interactions, you can keep working with pay-as-you-go credits rather than hitting a hard wall. Annual billing usually shaves a bit off the monthly rate.

## Free vs Paid: What Actually Changes

The **Free tier** is more than a demo. You get the full spec-driven workflow, agent hooks, and enough monthly interactions to build a small feature or evaluate whether the approach clicks for you. The ceiling is the interaction count — a handful of large agent runs can burn through a free allotment quickly.

Moving to **Pro** roughly 20x's your monthly interactions. That is the difference between "I tried Kiro on a weekend project" and "I use Kiro every workday." Pro is where most solo professionals land.

**Pro+** exists for developers who lean on the agent heavily — running many multi-step tasks per day, wiring up numerous hooks, or letting Kiro handle large refactors and test generation across a big codebase. The extra interactions cost roughly double Pro, but the per-interaction value improves.

Crucially, the *capabilities* (specs, hooks, model access) are consistent across tiers. You are paying for **volume of agent work**, not for unlocking features. That's a friendlier model than tools that paywall core functionality.

## Who Each Tier Suits

- **Free ($0):** Students, hobbyists, and anyone evaluating spec-driven development. Perfect for a proof of concept or a side project you touch occasionally.
- **Pro (~$19/mo):** The default for a working developer. If Kiro is part of your daily loop but you are one person shipping at a normal pace, Pro's interaction budget comfortably covers it.
- **Pro+ (~$39/mo):** Power users, contractors juggling multiple clients, and small teams that treat the agent as a full collaborator. If you routinely hit Pro's ceiling before month-end, Pro+ is cheaper than paying overages.

A quick gut check: estimate how many meaningful agent tasks you run per day, multiply by ~22 working days, and match that to a tier. If you are unsure, start on Pro and watch your usage before upgrading.

## Kiro vs Other AI IDEs

Kiro competes in a crowded field, and its pricing philosophy is distinct.

- **Vs Cursor:** Cursor is autocomplete- and chat-first, with fast inline edits and a large model menu. Kiro trades some of that immediacy for structure — specs make it stronger on larger, multi-file features you want to review before generation. Cursor's plans sit in a similar price band; see our [Cursor Pricing](/blog/cursor-pricing-2026) breakdown for a direct comparison.
- **Vs GitHub Copilot / others:** Copilot leans on per-seat flat pricing and shines at line-level suggestions. Kiro's interaction-metered model can be cheaper for light users (thanks to the free tier) but scales with heavy agent use.
- **Metering style:** Many rivals meter "requests" or "premium requests." Kiro's "agentic interaction" is closer to a unit of completed agent work, which can feel more predictable for spec-driven tasks but harder to compare apples-to-apples.

## Verdict

Kiro's pricing is refreshingly straightforward: a real free tier, Pro at ~$19/mo for daily individual use, and Pro+ at ~$39/mo for power users — all metered by agentic interactions rather than locked features. If your work benefits from spec-driven development and event-driven agent hooks, the value proposition is strong, especially since you can validate the workflow at zero cost before paying.

The main watch-out is interaction burn: large agent runs consume credits fast, so track your usage in the first month and size your tier accordingly. And because Kiro is still maturing out of preview, verify current limits and overage rates directly on Kiro's site before you commit.
