# Warp Pricing 2026: Complete Breakdown of Free, Pro, and Team Plans

Warp has evolved from a fast Rust-based terminal into an AI-powered command-line agent. In 2026 its pricing is built around AI request limits rather than seats alone. Here's exactly what Warp costs and which plan makes sense for you.

## Pricing Plans at a Glance

| Plan | Monthly Cost | AI Requests | Best For |
|------|-------------|-------------|----------|
| Free | $0 | ~150 requests/mo | Solo devs testing AI features |
| Pro | $18 ($15 annual) | ~2,500 requests/mo | Daily individual users |
| Team | $40/user | Pooled + higher limits | Small engineering teams |
| Enterprise | Custom | Custom + admin controls | Larger orgs |

## What Counts as an "AI Request"

The core of Warp's pricing is the AI request. One request is roughly one prompt to the agent — asking it to explain an error, generate a command, or run an autonomous coding task. Multi-step agent runs can consume several requests as the agent plans, executes, and self-corrects.

This matters because the free tier's ~150 requests disappear quickly once you start using Warp's agentic features rather than just autocomplete.

## Free Plan ($0)

The free plan includes the full terminal experience — blocks, workflows, themes, and a limited monthly allotment of AI requests. Everything that makes Warp a great terminal is free; only the volume of AI usage is capped.

**Best for:** Developers who want a modern terminal and occasional AI help without paying.

## Pro Plan ($18/month, or $15 billed annually)

Pro raises the AI request ceiling dramatically (~2,500/month) and unlocks priority access to the newest models. For most individual developers who lean on Warp's agent daily, this is the plan that removes the "am I about to run out?" anxiety.

**Best for:** Full-time developers using AI commands and agent tasks every day.

## Team Plan ($40/user/month)

The Team plan adds shared workflows, team drive for saved commands, centralized billing, and pooled AI requests across members. The per-seat cost is higher, but the collaboration features pay off once more than two or three engineers share conventions.

**Best for:** Engineering teams that want consistent tooling and shared command libraries.

## Enterprise Plan (Custom)

Enterprise brings SSO/SAML, SCIM provisioning, audit logs, zero-data-retention options, and volume discounts on AI requests. Pricing is quoted based on seat count and usage.

Warp's differentiator is the terminal itself: it's the tool you already live in, now with an agent attached. If your workflow is command-line heavy, Warp fits where an IDE plugin can't.

## Is Warp Worth Paying For?

### Pro pays off when:
- You use AI commands or agent runs multiple times a day
- You keep hitting the free tier's request cap
- Faster models measurably speed up your debugging

### Stick with free when:
- You mostly value the terminal UX, not the AI
- Your AI usage is a few prompts per week

## Tips to Optimize Warp Costs

1. **Reserve agent runs for real tasks** — casual questions still cost requests
2. **Use annual billing on Pro** — it drops the effective price to ~$15/month
3. **Pool requests on Team** — heavy and light users balance out
4. **Watch your usage meter** — Warp shows remaining requests in-app

## Bottom Line

Warp's free terminal is excellent and genuinely usable. The paid tiers are really about AI volume: if the agent has become part of your daily loop, Pro at ~$15/month annually is easy to justify. Teams get the most value from shared workflows once several engineers standardize on it.
