Acorns and Robinhood both target people who are new to investing — but they take completely different approaches. Acorns quietly invests your spare change in the background, while Robinhood puts you in the driver's seat with commission-free trading.

Neither is better in an absolute sense. The one that's right for you depends on how much involvement you want in managing your money.

## Acorns: Invest Without Thinking About It

Acorns is built around one idea: make investing invisible. Link your debit or credit card, and Acorns rounds up each purchase to the nearest dollar, sweeping the spare change into a diversified ETF portfolio.

Spend $4.60 on coffee? Acorns rounds up to $5.00 and invests $0.40. It's a genuinely effortless way to build a habit of investing, especially for people who find the stock market intimidating.

### What You Get with Acorns

- **Round-Ups**: Automatic micro-investments from everyday purchases
- **Pre-built portfolios**: Five risk-based portfolios (Conservative to Aggressive) built from low-cost ETFs
- **Acorns Later**: IRA account (Traditional, Roth, or SEP) available on Bronze+ plans
- **Acorns Checking**: A checking account with no overdraft fees and early direct deposit
- **Earn rewards**: Cash back from partner brands deposited directly into your investment account
- **Acorns Early**: Custodial investment accounts for kids (Gold plan)

### Acorns Pricing (2026)

| Plan | Price | What's Included |
|------|-------|----------------|
| Bronze | $3/month | Invest + checking |
| Silver | $6/month | + IRA |
| Gold | $9/month | + family accounts, custodial investing for kids |

One important note: the flat-fee structure can eat into returns when your balance is small. At $3/month on a $500 balance, you're paying 7.2% annually in fees — much higher than a typical ETF expense ratio. Acorns makes more sense as your balance grows into the thousands.

### Where Acorns Falls Short

- No individual stocks or ETF selection — you get what you're given
- Flat fee is expensive relative to small account balances
- Limited crypto exposure (only through portfolio allocations, not direct trading)
- You can't build a custom portfolio

## Robinhood: Trade What You Want, When You Want

Robinhood opened up commission-free stock trading to everyday investors and disrupted the brokerage industry in the process. The app lets you buy individual stocks, ETFs, options, and crypto without paying a trading fee — which was genuinely rare when Robinhood launched.

### What You Get with Robinhood

- **Commission-free trading**: Stocks, ETFs, and options with no per-trade commission
- **Fractional shares**: Invest in high-priced stocks like Amazon or Google with as little as $1
- **Crypto trading**: Buy and sell Bitcoin, Ethereum, and dozens of other cryptocurrencies
- **Robinhood Gold**: Premium subscription ($5/month) with higher interest rates on cash, margin investing, and Level II market data
- **Retirement IRA**: Traditional and Roth IRAs with a 1% match on contributions (3% with Gold)
- **Robinhood Gold Card**: A credit card that earns 3% cash back on all purchases

### Where Robinhood Falls Short

- Requires you to actively make decisions — there's no automation
- Options trading can be risky for inexperienced investors
- No pre-built portfolios or guided recommendations
- Customer support has been historically slow

## Fees: Flat vs Free

This is one of the clearest differences between the two apps.

Robinhood is free for standard use. There's no monthly fee to buy and sell stocks or ETFs. Robinhood Gold costs $5/month and adds margin investing, higher APY on uninvested cash, and other premium features — but it's entirely optional.

Acorns charges $3–$9/month regardless of how much you have invested. For small accounts, this is a significant ongoing cost. For accounts over $10,000–$20,000, the fee becomes less of a concern relative to portfolio size.

**If you're starting with a very small amount (under $1,000), Robinhood's free model has a clear cost advantage.**

## Account Types

Both apps offer individual taxable accounts and IRAs, but Acorns makes the IRA available starting at $6/month (Silver plan). Robinhood includes IRA access at no extra cost and even offers a contribution match.

Acorns adds custodial accounts for kids through its Gold plan, which Robinhood doesn't offer.

## Who Should Use Acorns?

Acorns could be a reasonable fit if you:

- Want investing to happen automatically without thinking about it
- Tend to forget to transfer money to savings or investment accounts
- Prefer a hands-off, "set it and forget it" approach
- Are building habits around saving and want a low-friction starting point
- Are investing for retirement or long-term goals with a risk-adjusted portfolio

## Who Should Use Robinhood?

Robinhood may suit you better if you:

- Want to pick your own stocks and ETFs
- Are interested in trading individual companies, not just diversified funds
- Want to invest in crypto alongside stocks
- Don't need round-ups or automated investing features
- Have at least some interest in learning how markets work

## Can You Use Both?

Some people use Robinhood for active investing and Acorns as a "background savings" tool. The round-up mechanism in Acorns works well as a complement to more intentional investing elsewhere. That said, if you're just starting out, picking one and sticking with it is usually the smarter move.

## The Verdict

If you want investing to happen passively, with minimal effort and decisions, **Acorns** may be a better starting point. The round-up mechanism genuinely removes friction, and the pre-built portfolios eliminate the paralysis of choosing what to buy.

If you're comfortable making decisions, or you want to learn how to invest in individual stocks and build your own portfolio, **Robinhood** is the stronger platform — especially given that it's free to use.

Neither app is a substitute for broader financial planning, and neither should be seen as a get-rich-quick tool. Both are reasonable entry points for beginners, just with very different philosophies about what "beginner-friendly" means.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Acorns or Robinhood better for beginners?

It depends on what kind of beginner you are. Acorns is better if you want a completely hands-off experience. Robinhood is better if you want to learn to pick investments yourself.

### Does Acorns charge fees?

Yes. Acorns charges $3/month (Bronze), $6/month (Silver), or $9/month (Gold). There are no free tiers.

### Is Robinhood actually free?

Standard Robinhood accounts have no monthly fee and no trading commissions on stocks and ETFs. Robinhood Gold costs $5/month for premium features, but it's optional.

### Can I lose money with Acorns?

Yes. Acorns invests in ETFs that go up and down with the market. Your balance can decrease, especially during market downturns.
