Glean has raised over $600M and is valued at $7.2 billion. It's one of the most well-funded enterprise AI companies in history. That doesn't automatically make it the right tool for your company — but it does mean the product is serious and the support organization behind it is stable.

Here's an honest look at what Glean actually does, where it works, and where the price tag becomes hard to defend.

## What Glean Is

Glean is an enterprise AI search platform. You connect it to your company's tools — Slack, Google Drive, Confluence, Salesforce, GitHub, Zendesk, Jira, and 100+ others — and it builds a unified, permission-respecting index of your company's knowledge. When your employees search for something, Glean pulls from all those sources and returns relevant results with AI-generated summaries.

It also includes:
- **Glean Chat**: A conversational AI interface that answers questions using your company's actual data, with source citations
- **Glean Agents**: AI automation workflows for repetitive tasks
- **Apps (formerly Assistants)**: Department-specific AI tools that can be built on top of Glean's knowledge base

The key differentiator is the permission model. Glean respects your existing access controls — if a sales rep shouldn't see an internal engineering doc, Glean won't surface it in their search results. For enterprise compliance, this matters enormously.

## Where Glean Performs Well

**Finding information across tools**: This is the core use case, and Glean is genuinely good at it. Typing a query that spans multiple systems — "what did we decide about the pricing model for Acme Corp?" — and getting relevant results from Slack threads, Confluence pages, and Salesforce notes in one place is a real productivity unlock.

**Onboarding new employees**: New hires spend a lot of time asking questions that are documented somewhere but hard to find. Glean's search surface reduces the friction of "where is this?" significantly. Glean cites 36 hours saved per new hire in onboarding — that's plausible for organizations with genuinely fragmented knowledge.

**Customer success and support teams**: If your support team needs to quickly find previous resolutions, account history, and internal documentation, Glean's cross-tool search reduces lookup time meaningfully.

**Technical teams**: Engineering teams working across GitHub, Confluence, Jira, and Slack find Glean particularly useful for pulling context without switching tools constantly.

## Where Glean Falls Short

**Knowledge quality depends on what's indexed**: Glean can only surface what exists and is written down somewhere. If your team's institutional knowledge lives in people's heads or in informal Slack conversations that get buried, Glean helps marginally. The search is as good as the content.

**Enterprise pricing with no self-serve option**: There's no free trial, no freemium tier, and no self-serve access. You need to go through a sales process to even test the product with real data. This is a legitimate friction point for evaluating ROI before buying.

**Overlaps with Microsoft Copilot**: For M365-heavy organizations, Microsoft Copilot ($30/user/month) covers a significant portion of what Glean does at a lower price point, integrated into tools people already use. Glean's edge is cross-platform breadth, but if 80% of your knowledge lives in SharePoint, Teams, and Outlook, Copilot is the more pragmatic choice.

**Implementation is not trivial**: Setting up Glean correctly requires IT involvement, connector configuration, permission review, and user adoption work. It's not a product you activate and immediately get value from. Budget 4–8 weeks for a proper rollout.

## What Reviewers Actually Say

The pattern in Glean reviews is consistent:
- **Positive**: Knowledge workers who used to spend 20–30 minutes finding information now spend 5 minutes. The multi-source synthesis is genuinely useful.
- **Negative**: Setup takes longer than expected. ROI takes 3–6 months to materialize. Some searches are excellent; others pull irrelevant results depending on how clean the source data is.
- **Universal**: It's expensive, and you need organizational buy-in to make it work.

G2 rating: 4.4/5 (based on 2026 reviews). Typical praise: "changed how we onboard" and "finally one place to search everything." Typical complaints: "took weeks to tune" and "expensive for what we use daily."

## Who Should Buy Glean

**Yes, evaluate Glean if:**
- 300+ employees across multiple tools (not just M365 or Google)
- Measurable time lost to "where is that document/conversation/decision?"
- Compliance-sensitive environment where permission-respecting search is required
- IT team available for implementation and ongoing connector maintenance

**No, skip Glean if:**
- Under 200 employees — cost-per-user is hard to recover
- Primarily M365 shop → Copilot is cheaper and integrated
- No dedicated IT for implementation
- Need to test before committing → no self-serve option is a blocker
- Budget under $100K/year for this category

## The Bottom Line

Glean is a capable, well-built product that solves a real enterprise problem — the fragmentation of company knowledge across too many tools. The AI search quality and permission model are genuinely differentiated compared to DIY solutions.

The challenge is the price and the enterprise-only model. At $45–$75/user, the software needs to demonstrably change how people work, not just make search slightly more convenient. For large, complex organizations where this problem is severe, that ROI case exists. For smaller or less-fragmented organizations, it's harder to justify.

Before engaging Glean's sales team, be clear on what problem you're actually solving and whether you can measure it. "Better search" is hard to justify at this price. "We lose X hours per week finding information across Y tools and that's measurable" is a different conversation.
