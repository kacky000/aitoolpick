# Qodo Pricing 2026: Complete Breakdown

If you are evaluating **Qodo pricing** in 2026, this guide breaks down every tier so you can pick the right plan without guesswork. Qodo (formerly Codium) is an AI code integrity platform focused on test generation, automated code review, and keeping your codebase reliable as it scales. Unlike autocomplete-first tools, Qodo's angle is quality: it writes tests, reviews pull requests, and flags integrity issues before they ship. Below you will find the Free (Developer) tier, the Teams plan at roughly $19/user/month, and the custom Enterprise option, plus who each tier suits and how it compares to Copilot and Cursor.

## What Qodo Is: Test Generation + Code Review

Qodo is not just another AI pair programmer. It is built around the idea that generating code is easy but *trusting* it is hard. The platform combines three core capabilities:

- **Test generation** — Qodo analyzes your functions and suggests meaningful unit tests, edge cases, and regression coverage automatically. This is its flagship feature and where the original Codium reputation came from.
- **Code review** — Qodo Merge (the PR agent) reviews pull requests, summarizes changes, suggests improvements, and catches bugs before a human reviewer ever opens the diff.
- **Code integrity** — Beyond individual suggestions, Qodo tracks whether changes preserve behavior, follow team standards, and stay consistent across the codebase.

These three pillars run inside your IDE (VS Code, JetBrains) and in your Git workflow (GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket), so the value shows up both while you write and when you merge.

## Free vs Teams vs Enterprise

Qodo follows a familiar three-tier structure, and the jump between tiers is mostly about collaboration, control, and scale rather than raw feature gates.

**Free (Developer)** targets individual developers who want to try test generation and basic review on personal or small projects. It includes IDE integration, test suggestions, and a capped amount of AI-assisted review usage each month.

**Teams (~$19/user/month)** is the sweet spot for engineering teams. It unlocks higher usage limits, team-wide code review automation via Qodo Merge, shared standards and rules, and priority model access. This is where the code integrity story becomes a real workflow rather than a solo convenience.

**Enterprise (custom)** adds security, deployment, and governance features that larger organizations require: SSO/SAML, self-hosted or VPC deployment options, audit logs, custom model routing, dedicated support, and volume-based pricing.

## Who Each Tier Suits

**Free (Developer)** is ideal if you are a solo developer, a student, or an engineer who wants to see whether AI-generated tests actually save time on your stack. You get enough to evaluate quality on real code without a credit card.

**Teams** fits startups and mid-size engineering teams that have moved past "does this work" and into "how do we keep quality consistent as we add people." At ~$19/user/month, the automated PR review and shared standards typically pay for themselves in reduced review time and fewer escaped bugs. If more than two engineers touch the same repo, this is the tier to budget for.

**Enterprise** is for organizations with compliance, security, or data-residency requirements — fintech, healthcare, government contractors, and any company where code cannot leave the perimeter. If you need SSO, self-hosting, and audit trails, the custom plan is the only realistic option.

## Qodo vs Copilot vs Cursor

Qodo occupies a different lane than most AI coding tools, so a straight price comparison can mislead.

- **GitHub Copilot** (around $10–$19/user/month for individual and business tiers) is primarily an autocomplete and chat assistant embedded in your editor. It is excellent at generating code quickly but is not built around test coverage or PR-level integrity. For a full breakdown, see [GitHub Copilot Pricing](/blog/github-copilot-pricing-2026).
- **Cursor** is an AI-native editor priced around $20/month for its Pro tier. It shines at large multi-file edits and agentic coding, but like Copilot, its center of gravity is *writing* code, not *verifying* it.
- **Qodo** at ~$19/user/month lands in the same price band but competes on a different axis: automated tests, review, and integrity. Many teams actually pair Qodo with Copilot or Cursor — using one to write faster and Qodo to make sure what got written is correct and tested.

That layering is worth emphasizing. If your bottleneck is typing speed, a Copilot-style tool wins. If your bottleneck is flaky tests, review backlog, or regressions slipping into main, Qodo's test and review focus is the better spend. ## Verdict

Qodo pricing in 2026 is straightforward and competitive: a genuinely useful Free tier, a Teams plan at roughly $19/user/month that unlocks the automated review and integrity workflow, and a custom Enterprise tier for governance-heavy organizations. What sets Qodo apart is not a lower price — it sits in the same range as Copilot and Cursor — but its focus on the part of development that usually gets skipped: writing tests and reviewing changes.

If you already use an autocomplete tool and keep shipping bugs or fighting weak test coverage, Qodo is a smart addition rather than a replacement. Start on the Free tier, confirm the generated tests match your standards, then move your team to the $19 plan once the review automation proves its worth. And before you commit budget, double-check the live numbers on Qodo's official site, since AI tool pricing changes fast.
