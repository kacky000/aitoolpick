Hootsuite dropped its free plan a few years back, and the pricing has only gone up since. If you're landing here after seeing $99/month on their checkout page and wondering if it's actually worth it — this breakdown will give you a straight answer.

## Hootsuite Pricing Plans in 2026

Hootsuite offers four tiers, all billed annually. There's no monthly billing option on standard plans, which is one of the first things that surprises new customers.

| Plan | Price (Annual) | Social Accounts | Users |
|------|---------------|-----------------|-------|
| Standard | $99/seat/month | Up to 10 | 1 |
| Professional | $199/seat/month | Up to 20 | 3 |
| Advanced | $399/seat/month | Up to 35 | 5 |
| Enterprise | Custom | Unlimited | Custom |

One thing that's easy to miss: these prices are **per seat**. If you have a team of three using Professional, you're looking at $597/month — not $199.

### Standard Plan ($99/month)

The entry-level plan gets you 10 social accounts, 1 user seat, scheduling, basic analytics, and access to Hootsuite's AI caption writer. For a solo social media manager handling multiple brand accounts, this might be workable. But the single-user limit means you can't loop in a client or colleague without upgrading.

What's missing at this tier: approval workflows, team assignments, and advanced reporting. You also lose access to Hootsuite's social listening features, which are locked to higher tiers.

### Professional Plan ($199/month)

This is where things start to make sense for small teams. You get 20 social accounts, 3 users, and access to approval workflows. If you're an agency managing client content and need sign-off before posts go live, this tier enables that workflow.

The analytics step up here too — you get access to 90 days of historical data instead of 30.

### Advanced Plan ($399/month)

Advanced unlocks 35 social accounts and 5 users. The bigger additions are the team performance reports and more granular analytics dashboards. At this price point, Hootsuite is competing head-to-head with Sprout Social.

The custom content approval flows and compliance features at this tier are primarily aimed at regulated industries — financial services, healthcare — where post review is mandatory, not just nice to have.

### Enterprise (Custom)

Enterprise pricing isn't published, but expect to start at four figures per month for larger teams. You get unlimited users, custom permissions, dedicated onboarding, and an account manager. For most businesses reading this, this tier isn't relevant unless you're a mid-market or larger organization.

## What You're Actually Getting for $99/Month

Let's be honest about the value proposition. Hootsuite is not cheap, and the feature set at the entry level reflects that the tool is built for professionals managing multiple accounts — not individual creators or small businesses posting to one Instagram account.

**Scheduling**: Hootsuite's bulk scheduling and auto-scheduling tools are genuinely good. You can upload a CSV of posts, and the platform will distribute them. The visual calendar interface is functional, though not the prettiest.

**Analytics**: At Standard, you get basic engagement metrics and some benchmarking against industry averages. It's useful but not deep. Most serious analytics work requires Professional or higher.

**Platform support**: You can connect Instagram, Facebook, X (Twitter), LinkedIn, TikTok, Pinterest, and YouTube. The integration depth varies — LinkedIn and Instagram tend to work best.

**AI tools**: The AI caption writer and "best time to post" suggestions are included across all paid plans. They're useful, not transformative.

## Hootsuite's Biggest Weaknesses in 2026

**No free plan**: This used to exist. It doesn't anymore. If you need to test the tool before committing, Hootsuite offers a 30-day free trial, which is better than nothing but requires a credit card.

**Per-seat pricing adds up fast**: A three-person team on Professional is $597/month. That's $7,164/year, which is a meaningful spend for most small businesses.

**The interface feels dated**: Hootsuite has been around since 2008, and parts of the UI reflect that. It's functional but not as clean as newer tools like Buffer or Later.

**Annual commitment required**: No monthly billing means you're locking in for a year. If your needs change — or the tool doesn't work out — you're stuck.

## Who Should Actually Pay for Hootsuite?

**It makes sense for:**
- Social media agencies managing 10+ client accounts
- Marketing teams at mid-sized companies that need approval workflows
- Regulated industries needing compliance features
- Teams already deep in the Hootsuite ecosystem that have built workflows around it

**It doesn't make sense for:**
- Individual creators or solo entrepreneurs
- Small businesses with 1-3 social accounts
- Teams that primarily need scheduling without heavy analytics
- Anyone on a tight budget — there are dramatically cheaper options

## Cheaper Alternatives Worth Considering

If $99/month is hard to justify, there are two tools worth looking at seriously.

[Buffer](https://aitoolpick.org/compare/buffer-vs-hootsuite/) starts with a free plan covering 3 channels and goes up to $6-$12 per channel per month. For most small teams, that's $30-$60/month total — a fraction of Hootsuite's entry price.

[Sprout Social](https://aitoolpick.org/compare/sprout-social-vs-hootsuite/) starts at $199/seat and is actually more expensive than Hootsuite's Standard plan, but the analytics and reporting depth is noticeably better. For teams where data and client reporting matter, Sprout often wins.

## The Bottom Line

Hootsuite at $99/month is defensible if you're managing 5+ social accounts, working with a team, and need scheduling plus decent analytics. It's hard to justify for small teams or individuals.

The core problem is the all-or-nothing annual commitment combined with per-seat pricing. By the time you add users, costs escalate quickly.

If you're evaluating social media tools, try Buffer's free plan first to validate your workflow. If you consistently hit Buffer's limits and need team features, then Hootsuite at Standard is a reasonable next step. If budget allows and analytics depth matters, Sprout Social is worth the higher entry price.

Hootsuite isn't a bad tool. It's just priced for a specific customer, and that customer isn't everyone.
