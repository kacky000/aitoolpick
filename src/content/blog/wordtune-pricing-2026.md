# Wordtune Pricing 2026: Complete Breakdown

If you write emails, essays, or marketing copy and want your sentences to sound sharper, understanding Wordtune pricing is the first step before you commit. Wordtune is an AI writing and rewriting assistant that reshapes your existing sentences rather than generating content from scratch. This 2026 breakdown covers every plan, realistic costs, who each tier suits, and how Wordtune stacks up against Grammarly and QuillBot so you can choose confidently.

## What Wordtune Is

Wordtune is best known for one thing: rewriting. You highlight a sentence, and Wordtune offers multiple alternative phrasings that keep your meaning but change the flow. It's less about writing a blog post for you and more about polishing what you already wrote.

Core features include:

- **Rewrite**: Get several rewordings of any sentence with a single click.
- **Tones**: Switch between casual and formal registers, or make a line shorter or longer.
- **Summarizer**: Condense long articles, PDFs, and YouTube videos into key points.
- **AI writing add-ons**: Spices and templates that add statistics, examples, jokes, or counterarguments to expand your draft.

It works as a browser extension (Chrome, Edge, Safari), inside Google Docs, in Microsoft Word, and through its own web editor. That everywhere-you-type convenience is a big part of the appeal.

## Free vs Paid Tiers

Wordtune offers a genuinely usable free plan, which is unusual in this category. The Free tier gives you a limited number of rewrites and AI prompts per day, enough to test the workflow and cover light, occasional editing. If you polish only a few emails a week, Free may be all you ever need.

Paid tiers unlock higher (or unlimited) daily rewrites, more generative AI prompts, longer summaries, and premium tones. The jump from Free to a paid plan is really a jump from "a handful of rewrites a day" to "edit as much as you want without hitting a wall."

## Wordtune Pricing Table (2026)

| Plan | Approx. Price (billed annually) | Rewrites | Key features |
|------|-------------------------------|----------|--------------|
| **Free** | $0 | Limited (a few per day) | Basic rewrite, limited tones, short summaries |
| **Advanced / Plus** | ~$10–14/mo | Higher daily cap | More rewrites, expanded AI prompts, all tones, summarizer |
| **Unlimited** | ~$16–20/mo | Unlimited | Unlimited rewrites, unlimited AI prompts, full summarizer |
| **Business / Teams** | Custom (per seat) | Unlimited | Team billing, admin controls, centralized management, priority support |

> **Note:** These figures are estimates for 2026 and vary by billing cycle (monthly costs more than annual) and promotions. Always verify the current numbers on Wordtune's official pricing page before purchasing.

Annual billing is where the value sits: paying monthly typically raises the effective price meaningfully, so if you know you'll use Wordtune for the year, the annual commitment is the cheaper route.

## Who Each Tier Suits

**Free** — Perfect for students, casual users, and anyone who wants to try AI rewriting risk-free. If your editing needs are light and sporadic, the daily rewrite cap rarely gets in the way.

**Advanced / Plus** — The sweet spot for regular writers: freelancers, professionals sending lots of email, and non-native English speakers who want confident phrasing. You get enough rewrites and AI prompts for daily use without paying for "unlimited."

**Unlimited** — For heavy writers, content creators, and copywriters who edit for hours every day. If you constantly hit the Advanced cap, Unlimited pays for itself in saved friction.

**Business / Teams** — Aimed at agencies, marketing departments, and companies that want consistent tone across writers plus centralized billing and admin controls.

## Wordtune vs Grammarly and QuillBot

**Wordtune vs Grammarly**: Grammarly leads on grammar, spelling, and correctness checking, with a broader AI writing suite. Wordtune leads on *rephrasing quality* — its rewrite suggestions tend to feel more natural and varied. Many writers run both: Grammarly for correctness, Wordtune for flow. On price, they land in a similar premium range, so the choice comes down to whether you value error-catching or sentence-reshaping more.

**Wordtune vs QuillBot**: QuillBot is the value pick. Its paraphrasing tool is strong and often cheaper, and it bundles a grammar checker, summarizer, and citation tools. Wordtune's interface and tone controls feel more polished, and its in-context rewrites integrate more smoothly as you type. If budget is the priority, QuillBot; if you want the cleanest rewriting UX, Wordtune.

## Verdict

Wordtune earns its place if rewriting and tone are your main pain points. The Free tier is legitimately useful, which makes trying it a no-brainer, and the Advanced plan around $10–14/mo (billed annually) is the best-value entry point for most everyday writers. Reserve Unlimited for genuinely heavy daily editing, and Business only when you're managing a team.

It won't replace a full generator like Jasper or Copy.ai for producing content from nothing, and it isn't the last word on grammar the way Grammarly is. But as a focused, elegant rewriting assistant, Wordtune is one of the strongest tools in its lane in 2026. Just confirm current prices on the official site before you buy, since tiers and promotions shift.
