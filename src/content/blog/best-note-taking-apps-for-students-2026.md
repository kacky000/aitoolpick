The best note-taking app is the one you'll actually use consistently through a semester. With that in mind, here are the tools that consistently earn high marks from students in 2026 — from free offline-first options to connected cloud platforms.

## Quick Picks

- **Best overall free**: Obsidian
- **Best for collaboration**: Notion
- **Best for researchers**: Logseq
- **Best simple option**: Apple Notes / Google Keep
- **Best for med/law students**: Notion or Obsidian with templates

## 1. Obsidian — Best Free Option for Serious Students

**Price**: Free for personal use (Sync addon: $4/month)

Obsidian stores all your notes as plain Markdown files on your own device. There's no subscription required to access the core editor, and it works completely offline.

**Why students love it:**
- **Bidirectional links** create a web of connected ideas — perfect for building knowledge across courses
- **Graph view** visualizes how your notes relate to each other
- **Huge plugin ecosystem** — flashcards (Spaced Repetition plugin), PDF annotation, LaTeX math
- **Zero lock-in** — your notes are text files you own forever

**Drawbacks**: The learning curve is steep. It takes a week or two to internalize the link-based workflow. Mobile app is functional but less polished than the desktop experience.

**Best for**: STEM students, researchers, anyone taking notes across multiple interconnected subjects.

## 2. Notion — Best for Organization and Collaboration

**Price**: Free (Plus: $10/month, though most students only need free)

Notion's free tier is remarkably generous for students. You get unlimited pages, databases, and the ability to share documents with classmates for group projects.

**Why students love it:**
- **Databases** for organizing courses, assignments, and deadlines
- **Templates** for every academic use case (Cornell Notes, study schedules, reading trackers)
- **Real-time collaboration** for group projects and study groups
- **All-in-one** — notes, to-do lists, project management in one place

**Drawbacks**: Notion can be slow with large page collections. The free tier limits some AI features. It requires internet for most workflows.

**Best for**: Students who need to organize many different aspects of their academic life, or anyone working on group projects.

## 3. Logseq — Best for Research-Heavy Work

**Price**: Free and open-source (Sync: $5/month)

Logseq takes an outliner approach to note-taking — every note is a bullet point, and everything is linked. It's particularly powerful for synthesizing information across multiple sources.

**Why students love it:**
- **Journal-first workflow** — daily notes automatically organize over time
- **Block-level links** — link to specific paragraphs, not just whole pages
- **Queries** — filter and surface notes by tags, dates, or properties
- **PDF annotation** built in
- **Completely free** for local use

**Drawbacks**: The outliner format doesn't suit everyone. Steeper learning curve than Notion. Smaller community than Obsidian.

**Best for**: Graduate students, researchers, and anyone doing literature reviews or deep research projects.

## 4. Evernote — Declining but Still Functional

**Price**: Free (very limited, 50 notes max), Starter $10.83/month

Evernote built its reputation on the best web clipper in the market and excellent OCR for photos of handwritten notes. Unfortunately, its pricing has become aggressive and the free tier is now severely restricted.

At 50 notes maximum, the free tier is only useful for a trial. Students are better served by Notion's free tier (unlimited) or Obsidian (completely free).

**Best for**: If you're already paying for it, the web clipper and image OCR remain best-in-class. For new students, skip it.

## 5. Apple Notes / Google Keep — Underrated Simple Options

**Price**: Free (built into iOS/macOS or Google account)

Don't overlook the apps you already have. Apple Notes added significant features in recent years — handwriting-to-text, quick notes from the lock screen, smart folders, PDF scanning. Google Keep excels at visual notes and integration with Google Docs/Drive.

**Best for**: Students who need a frictionless place to capture ideas quickly, or anyone whose workflow lives primarily in the Apple or Google ecosystem.

## Choosing Based on Your Study Style

| Study Style | Recommended Tool |
|---|---|
| Lecture notes + flashcards | Obsidian (with Anki plugin) |
| Group projects + collaboration | Notion |
| Research papers + citation tracking | Logseq |
| Quick capture on phone | Apple Notes / Google Keep |
| Pre-med / law / heavy reading | Notion or Logseq |
| Engineering / math heavy | Obsidian (LaTeX plugins) |

## The Free Stack That Works

Most students don't need to pay anything. A practical free setup:

1. **Obsidian** (free, offline) for permanent course notes and connected knowledge
2. **Notion** (free) for project management, group work, and scheduling
3. **Google Keep** for quick capture on the go

This combination covers the full note-taking lifecycle without spending a dollar.
