Enterprise AI search is one of the fastest-growing software categories in 2026. Every major vendor is pitching some version of "ask your company's AI anything and get an answer from your internal data." The products vary enormously in quality, scope, and price.

Here's an honest comparison of the tools worth evaluating if you're responsible for improving how your organization finds and uses internal knowledge.

## What Enterprise AI Search Actually Is

Enterprise AI search is different from consumer AI assistants (ChatGPT, Claude). The key distinction: enterprise search indexes your company's actual data — Slack, Confluence, Salesforce, GitHub, Google Drive — and uses AI to answer questions using that data with proper permission controls.

The value proposition: instead of searching five different tools to find what you're looking for, you ask one interface and get an answer that cites sources from wherever the relevant information actually lives.

The catch: it only works if the knowledge exists somewhere in your connected systems. AI can't surface information that was never written down.

## Top Enterprise AI Search Tools in 2026

### Glean — Best for Large, Multi-Tool Organizations

Glean connects to 100+ enterprise tools and builds a unified, permission-respecting knowledge graph. The standout capabilities are cross-platform synthesis (finding information that spans multiple tools) and the permission model that ensures users only see content they're already authorized to access.

**Best for**: Organizations 300+ with fragmented knowledge across many tools  
**Pricing**: $45–$75/user/month (negotiated enterprise contracts)  
**Weakness**: No self-serve trial; expensive for smaller teams

See the full [Glean pricing 2026](/blog/glean-pricing-2026/) and [Glean review 2026](/blog/glean-review-2026/) for details.

### Microsoft Copilot — Best for M365-Heavy Organizations

If your organization runs on Microsoft 365, Copilot is the native answer. At $30/user/month added to an M365 subscription, it searches across Teams, SharePoint, Outlook, and Office files with direct integration into the apps your employees already use.

**Best for**: Organizations where 70%+ of knowledge lives in Microsoft tools  
**Pricing**: $30/user/month (on top of M365 subscription)  
**Weakness**: Weaker at pulling from non-Microsoft tools (Slack, Salesforce, GitHub)

### Notion AI — Best for Notion-Centric Teams

If your team already uses Notion as the primary knowledge base, Notion AI is the most affordable way to add conversational AI to your internal documentation. At $10/user/month as an add-on, it answers questions from your Notion workspace content.

**Best for**: Teams that have invested in Notion as their knowledge management system  
**Pricing**: $10/user/month (Notion AI add-on)  
**Weakness**: Only searches Notion content, not other tools

### Guru — Best for Customer-Facing Teams

Guru is a knowledge base purpose-built for sales, support, and customer success teams. It stores verified, expert-authored knowledge and delivers it in the context of other tools (as a browser extension or embedded in Salesforce/Zendesk).

**Best for**: Sales and support teams where knowledge accuracy is critical  
**Pricing**: $18–$20/user/month  
**Weakness**: Not designed for general enterprise search across all tools

### Confluence + Atlassian Intelligence — Best for Engineering/Tech Teams

For engineering and product teams already in the Atlassian ecosystem (Jira + Confluence), Atlassian Intelligence adds AI capabilities to search and summarization within those tools.

**Best for**: Engineering-driven organizations in the Atlassian ecosystem  
**Pricing**: Included in Confluence Cloud Premium ($57.50/user/month for the suite) or available as add-on  
**Weakness**: Limited to Atlassian suite; not a cross-tool solution

## How to Decide

The selection framework is actually simple once you're honest about where your organization's knowledge actually lives:

**If 70%+ of knowledge is in Microsoft tools**: Copilot. It's cheaper, integrated, and improving rapidly.

**If you're Notion-first with a focused knowledge base**: Notion AI add-on. Cheapest, frictionless, fits the existing system.

**If knowledge is genuinely fragmented across Salesforce, Slack, GitHub, Google Workspace, and Confluence simultaneously**: Glean. This is the specific problem it's designed for.

**If you need verified, accurate knowledge for customer-facing teams**: Guru. The editorial verification workflow ensures accuracy in a way that pure AI search doesn't.

**If you're in the Atlassian ecosystem**: Atlassian Intelligence as part of your existing Confluence Premium subscription.

## Questions to Ask Before Buying

1. **Where does knowledge actually live?** Not where you wish it lived — where it actually is. Survey your team.

2. **Is the problem search friction or knowledge quality?** AI search makes good knowledge easier to find. It doesn't fix sparse, outdated, or missing documentation. Solve the knowledge quality problem first.

3. **Can you measure the current cost of the problem?** "It takes too long to find things" is hard to justify at enterprise software prices. "Our analysis shows 45 minutes/day lost per knowledge worker × 500 employees × $60/hour fully loaded" is a real business case.

4. **Will employees actually use it?** New search tools compete with deeply embedded habits of asking colleagues on Slack, searching Google, or checking the tool they know. Adoption is always the hardest part.

5. **What does IT say?** Connecting these tools to your systems involves security reviews, SSO integration, and data handling agreements. Get IT in the room early.

## The Bottom Line

Enterprise AI search is a real category with real value — the fragmentation of company knowledge across too many tools is a genuine productivity drag. The tools above address it in different ways at very different prices.

Don't buy based on demos and promise. Run a proof-of-concept with real data, measure whether employees actually use it, and calculate whether the search behavior change justifies the annual contract.

The best tool is the one your team actually uses to find information — not the most feature-rich one in a sales presentation.
